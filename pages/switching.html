Coming from Lightroom or Capture One.

Where Rawtonic stands against the better of the two today, honestly, then what comes with you and where things are.

Where Rawtonic stands today

Rawtonic’s rule is to match or beat the better of Lightroom Classic and Capture One in every tool. It does not yet, everywhere. In short, from our side-by-side tests (development builds shortly before alpha.4) and the measurements of alpha.6 and alpha.7 (the numbers, and what each covers, are below):

What works today

  • Auto white balance finds the colour of the light more accurately than Camera Raw’s Auto on our test set.
  • Importing a card with a backup copy is faster than in Lightroom and Capture One: 200 RAW+JPEG pairs in about 10 s (Lightroom 11.5 s, Capture One 23.7 s on the same laptop).
  • Sliders keep following your hand while a batch export runs.
  • Merging an HDR bracket is faster than in Lightroom.
  • Photos moved or renamed outside the program are found again, with their ratings.
  • A tethered shot is on screen as quickly as in Capture One on the Sony α7 III, α7 IV and Fujifilm X-H2S.
  • Auto straighten usually gets the horizon closer than Lightroom.

Not there yet

  • Stepping through a shoot has not been compared with Lightroom and Capture One by real key presses since it was reworked in alpha.6. In scripted runs the next photo shows after about 90 ms and is fully developed after about 0.2 s; stepping faster than about every 0.6 s, the camera’s own JPEG shows within about 20 ms and the developed photo follows after about 0.4 s.
  • A batch export took about twice as long as in Lightroom or Capture One when they were last compared (alpha.4). It has become faster since (on a Mac mini, about 25 s instead of about 41 s for the same export), but has not been compared with them again.
  • The very first AI subject mask after installing still waits for its model if you click within about 15 s of opening Develop. A click 25 s in took 1.5–3.3 s (Lightroom’s first use: 7.9 s); after that it takes under a second.
  • AI Denoise is slower than Lightroom’s and its result is a little less accurate.
  • With a Fujifilm GFX 50R, tethered shots arrive a little later than in Capture One.
  • Auto straighten’s rare misses are a little larger than Lightroom’s.
  • A project’s photos cannot be moved to another drive one by one yet; move the whole folder instead.
The measurements behind this

These are the measurements behind the lists above, worse results included. Unless noted: one MacBook Pro (Apple M5 Pro), the same files in all three programs, Lightroom Classic 15.6 and Capture One 16.6, measured between 10 and 24 October 2026. Rawtonic was a development build of each day, before alpha.4; the rows culling, slider during export and typed value used commit 20c8c71d. Rows measured again since say so: import with the alpha.6 code (Rawtonic only, on the same laptop and card), culling and batch export with alpha.6 and alpha.7 on a Mac mini (Apple M4 Pro), where Lightroom and Capture One were not measured. Changes after alpha.7 are not measured here. Where one program was not measured, the verdict compares only with the other. Times come from screen recordings at 60 frames a second, so they are known to about one frame (0.017 s): they show when the screen changed, not the full delay from hand to eye.

What comes with you

  • Your library. “Import from Lightroom or Capture One…”, on the start screen or in the library’s menu, reads a Lightroom Classic catalog (.lrcat) or a Capture One session (.cosessiondb): ratings, colour labels, keywords, edits and albums. The other library is only read, never changed. If its photos moved, point Rawtonic to their new folder.
  • Presets and styles. Import Lightroom presets and Capture One styles in Develop’s Styles & presets panel. Rawtonic lists what it could not translate; Capture One’s Color Editor becomes Rawtonic’s colour editor.
  • Sidecars. Ratings, labels and keywords in XMP sidecars are read when photos are added; Lightroom’s own settings in a sidecar are kept untouched when Rawtonic writes to it.
  • Not yet: virtual copies, smart albums and Capture One’s local adjustment layers are listed as not imported. Not every adjustment looks the same as in the program it came from.
