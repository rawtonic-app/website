Masks and layers.

A layer is a second set of adjustments that acts only where its mask says: brighten a face, darken a sky, warm a background. Each layer has its own sliders (exposure, colour, sharpening and most other tools) and a mask built from one or more parts.

{{shot "masks-layers-panel" "the Layers tab with a sky and a subject layer, the mask shown as an overlay"}}

Working with layers

  • New layer (+) in the Layers tab, or in Layers & masks at the top of the Adjust tab, offers every kind of mask below. While a layer is selected, the sliders of every tab act on it; Background goes back to the whole photo.
  • A mask can combine parts: Add, Subtract or Intersect each one, and Invert a part or the whole mask. For example: Sky, then subtract a brush where a tree reaches into it.
  • M shows or hides the mask over the photo, ⌥M (Alt+M) shows it in grey, H shows only the selected layer’s effect. Mask overlay settings set its colour and when it shows.
  • Up to 32 visible layers are applied to a photo; hide or delete one to make room.

Draw: brush and gradients

Brush
B paints the mask, E erases from it; ⌘↑ and ⌘↓ (Ctrl+↑, Ctrl+↓) change the size. Size, hardness, flow and opacity are in the brush’s settings (right-click the photo). Auto mask keeps the stroke to the edge under the brush, so you can paint a wall without colouring the sky beside it. With a pressure-sensitive pen, pressing harder can paint wider or stronger.
Linear gradient
L: drag across the photo for an effect that fades from one side, the graduated filter for a bright sky. A two-sided band is possible too.
Radial gradient
T: an ellipse that fades towards its edge, inside or outside, to lead the eye or make your own vignette.

Brush and eraser also work on gradient and AI layers: paint in or take out a bit, and the gradient stays editable.

Range: colour, luminance and depth

  • Color range: click a colour in the photo or drag over an area (Shift adds up to five samples); the mask takes every place of that colour, with Refine for how wide.
  • Luminance range: tones from shadows to highlights, with a soft falloff. Limit to a luma range… does the same for any other mask, for example only the bright part of a gradient.
  • Depth range: near and far limits in the scene’s depth, estimated by an AI model from the photo, for example to calm a busy background.

Select: AI selections

Subject and Background
The main subject, or everything but it. ⇧, and ⇧. make a new subject or background layer at once.
Sky
The sky, for a deeper blue or calmer clouds without touching the land.
People
Each person, or everyone, as a whole or in parts: face skin, body skin, hair, eyebrows, iris and pupil, eye sclera, lips, teeth, clothes.
Landscape
Parts of a landscape to pick from: sky, mountains, vegetation, water, natural ground, artificial ground, architecture. A part the photo does not have is shown as not found.
Object
Click an object or drag a box around it; Shift-click adds another, Alt-click leaves a part out.

Refine edge lets the mask follow the photo’s own edge within a radius you set, so hair, fur and leaves keep their soft outline. The AI models run on your computer, never on a server; each downloads the first time you use it.

AI selections are made for each photo: paste, sync or apply a preset with a sky layer to fifty photos, and each gets its own sky, also photos you never opened.

On computers with little memory

AI selections need a lot of memory for a large photo. On a computer with 16 GB or less, Rawtonic starts one only when there is room for it: until then its layer says “waiting for memory…”, and it is computed by itself once memory is free (closing other programs or switching to a single photo view helps). On Windows and Linux computers that run AI on the processor, a Subject or Background selection that would not fit runs in a slower mode that needs less memory, and its layer says so. In our test with a 50-megapixel RAW on a 14 GB Windows laptop, Subject selections stayed within Rawtonic’s memory limit in 11 of 11 runs; other computers and photos are not measured yet.

If you come from Lightroom: layers are Lightroom’s masks, with the same kinds and Add, Subtract and Intersect. From Capture One: a layer is a Capture One layer, with its mask built from parts; Capture One’s layers with gradients come over in imports as editable gradients, brush strokes as fixed masks.
