Develop.

Develop is where a RAW file becomes a photograph. Every change is a setting stored with the photo, never a change to the file itself: you can go back to any earlier state, compare it with the original, and carry it to other photos.

{{shot "develop-overview" "Develop with the Adjust tab open beside a portrait, filmstrip below"}}

The tabs

The tools sit in six tabs at the top of the tool column (left or right of the photo, as you chose at the start; Settings → General changes it):

Adjust
The everyday tools: Styles & presets, profile, white balance, Exposure (Basic tone), Presence, tone curve, and effects such as vignette and light effects. Many edits never leave this tab.
Color
The colour mixer, colour editor, colour grading, black & white and calibration: see Colour.
Detail
Sharpening, noise reduction (with AI Denoise), grain, defringe and moiré: see Detail.
Crop
Crop, straighten, perspective and lens correction: see Crop and geometry.
Layers
Local adjustments with brushes, gradients and AI selections: see Masks and layers.
Retouch
Spot removal, object removal and face retouch.

Each tool’s header has a switch to turn the tool off and on and a reset; holding the reset (or pressing it with ⌥/Alt) shows the photo without that tool until you let go. ⌥/Alt-click a header to open only that tool; Right-click a header to move the tool up or down, or to turn on Solo Mode, which keeps one tool open at a time. Auto (⌘L, Ctrl+L) sets the tone for you as a starting point.

Sliders

Drag a slider, or its name, to change it; double-click either to reset it; click the number to type a value. A small tick under each slider marks its default. Press and hold a slider’s name for a quarter of a second to see the photo with just that slider at its default; let go and your setting is back exactly. Nothing changes and no history step is added, so it is a quick “what does this slider do here?”.

Before and after

  • Hold \ to see the whole photo as it was before your edits; let go to return. ⌘\ (Ctrl+\) keeps the before until you press it again.
  • Y turns on the before/after view; ⇧Y steps through its modes: split left/right, split top/bottom, side by side.
  • Instead of the unedited photo, a snapshot can be the “before”: choose Compare on the snapshot.
  • J shows the clipping warnings: where highlights or shadows lose their detail.

History and snapshots

⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) undoes, ⇧⌘Z or ⌘Y (Ctrl+Y) redoes. History, at the bottom of the tool column, lists the steps of the open photo; click one to go back to it. Rawtonic keeps the last 50 steps of each of the last 100 photos you edited while it is open.

Snapshots keep an edit under a name for good: + saves the current edit, the camera beside a history step saves that step. Restoring a snapshot is itself a step that Undo takes back, so trying a snapshot never loses your current edit. Snapshots are kept with the photo in the library.

Carrying an edit to other photos

Copy and paste
⇧⌘C (Ctrl+Shift+C) opens Copy adjustments: tick the tools to copy (Changed picks only the tools changed on this photo) and whether local layers come along. ⇧⌘V (Ctrl+Shift+V) pastes onto the selected photos. On Windows and Linux, Ctrl+C in Develop copies the same tools as last time without the dialog.
From the previous photo
⌥⌘V (Ctrl+Alt+V) pastes the edit of the photo you had open before, the quickest way through a series shot in the same light.
Sync
Select several photos in the filmstrip and press ⇧⌘S (Ctrl+Shift+S): the open photo’s edit goes to all of them, with the same choice of tools as Copy.
One tool
Each tool’s copy button copies or applies just that tool, for example only the white balance.

Pasted layers join each photo’s own. AI selections such as Sky or Subject are detected again on each photo, so a pasted sky mask fits that photo’s sky.

Reset Adjustments (⇧⌘R, Ctrl+Shift+R) goes back to the photo’s develop defaults; Reset to Neutral goes back to no adjustments at all.

Exporting from Develop

⇧⌘E (Ctrl+Shift+E) opens the Export dialog; Export with Previous (⌥⇧⌘E, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+E) exports the selected photos again with the settings of the last export, without the dialog. See Export.

If you come from Lightroom or Capture One: the keys for before/after (\, Y), copy and paste (⇧⌘C, ⇧⌘V), sync and reset are the ones you know. Two things are new: holding a slider’s name to see its default, and holding a tool’s reset to see the photo without the tool.
