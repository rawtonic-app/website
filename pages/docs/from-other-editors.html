From Lightroom or Capture One.

You do not have to start over. Rawtonic reads a Lightroom Classic catalog (.lrcat) or a Capture One session (.cosessiondb) or catalog (.cocatalog) and brings your ratings, labels, keywords, albums and edits into a Rawtonic catalog. The other program’s library is only read, never changed, and your photos stay where they are.

{{shot "catalog-import" "the Import from Lightroom or Capture One dialog with the counts of a checked catalog"}}

How to import

  1. Choose Import from Lightroom or Capture One… on the start screen or in the library menu. Rawtonic first makes a new catalog for it (or imports into the catalog that is open).
  2. Pick the catalog or session file and click Check. Rawtonic shows what it found before anything happens: photos, how many with edits, albums, virtual copies, keywords, and what it will leave out.
  3. If photos moved since the other program last saw them, point to their new folder beside each folder it lists.
  4. Click Import into …. Afterwards, the photos are in a Previous Import album.

What translates

  • Library: ratings, flags, colour labels, keywords with their hierarchy, albums, smart albums whose rules Rawtonic can read, virtual copies and snapshots.
  • Edits: the global adjustments, crop and geometry, lens correction, and local adjustments. Sky, Subject and Landscape masks are selected again on the photo by Rawtonic’s own AI selections; gradients stay editable gradients.
  • Presets and styles import separately, in Develop’s Styles & presets panel.
  • Sidecars: Rawtonic reads ratings, labels and keywords from XMP sidecars and keeps the other program’s own settings in a sidecar untouched when it writes to it.

What does not translate yet

  • Lightroom’s spot removal, history steps and the names of virtual copies; keyword synonyms and smart albums with rules Rawtonic cannot read (the check lists them).
  • Capture One’s brush strokes, colour ranges and AI selections come over as fixed masks, not as strokes or ranges you can edit.
  • Not every adjustment looks exactly as it did in the program it came from; check the photos that matter before you deliver them.

Where things are

Most tools have the names you know. A few are called differently: Lightroom’s Masking and Capture One’s layers are Layers, presets and styles are Styles & presets, an export preset or process recipe is an Export recipe, Lightroom’s Survey view is the Multi view (N). The full list, and where Rawtonic stands against both programs, is on Switching.
