Detail.

The Detail tab holds the tools that work at the scale of single pixels: sharpening, noise reduction, grain, and fixes for colour fringes and moiré. Judge them at 100 % (Z); at Fit you see little of what they do.

{{shot "detail-sharpening" "the Sharpening group beside a 100 % view of an eye"}}

Sharpening

Every RAW file is a little soft from the sensor and its filters; sharpening brings back the crispness the scene had.

Amount
How strongly edges are sharpened, up to 150.
Radius
How wide the sharpened edge is, from 0.5 to 3 pixels. Small for fine detail such as landscapes and fabric, larger for soft subjects.
Detail
How much of the finest texture is sharpened as well, compared with clear edges only. High values bring out texture, and noise with it.
Masking
Leaves smooth areas such as skin and sky out of the sharpening, so only edges get it. Raise it for portraits.
Threshold
Leaves out differences smaller than this, so faint noise is not sharpened.
Falloff
Sharpens the corners more than the centre, for lenses that are softer at the edges.

On a layer, sharpening can also be negative, to soften one part of the photo, for example a background. For sharpening that fits print or screen at the final size, use the output sharpening in Export.

Noise reduction

Noise reduction has two kinds of noise to treat. Luminance smooths the grainy speckle of high-ISO photos, with Detail and Contrast to keep fine structure. Color removes the coloured blotches, with Detail and Smoothness. Use luminance noise reduction with care: it always costs some fine detail.

For high-ISO photos, AI Denoise removes noise before the RAW is turned into colours, keeping far more detail than smoothing afterwards can. It has its own page: AI Denoise.

Grain

Grain adds a film-like texture: Amount, Size and Roughness, a grain colour, and a structure (even, soft, clumped, gritty or speckled). It is drawn at the photo’s own scale, so the exported file has the grain you see at 100 %. A little grain also hides banding in smooth skies after strong edits.

Colour fringes and moiré

  • Defringe removes the purple and green fringes that some lenses leave along high-contrast edges, such as branches against a bright sky. The lateral colour fringes in the corners are corrected with the lens instead: see Lens correction.
  • Moiré removes the coloured wavy patterns that fine fabric or screens can make on sensors without an anti-alias filter.

If you come from Lightroom: Amount, Radius, Detail and Masking are Lightroom’s sliders with the same ranges. From Capture One: Threshold and Falloff are there as in Capture One’s sharpening.
