Version 0.1.0-alpha.2, the second alpha · macOS 13+ on Apple Silicon · Linux x86-64

Originals stay yours.

Your RAW files are never modified or deleted. Ingest verifies every copy; edits live in a session and XMP sidecars.

Fast, on the graphics card.

The photograph is rendered on the GPU, so sliders, brushes and masks respond while you drag.

Private by design.

No account, no telemetry, no update checks. The optional AI tools run on your computer, never in the cloud.

From card to client.

Find the keepers.

Ingest verifies every copy, with an optional second backup, or drag photos straight in from the Finder or your file manager. Rate, pick, filter and sort the shoot; metadata travels in XMP sidecars.

Shape the light.

Exposure, highlights, shadows, whites and blacks, levels, point and region curves, clarity, structure and dehaze. Sharpening, noise reduction, lens corrections, crop and perspective.

Find the feeling.

RAW white balance, a colour editor, three-way colour grading, vibrance, a black-and-white mix, camera profiles, CUBE LUTs and soft proofing for print and web colour spaces.

Work on one part.

Brush, linear and radial masks, colour and luminance ranges, and masks for subject, sky, background, depth and people: skin, hair, eyes, lips and clothes, each with its own opacity.

Clean it up.

Heal and clone with spots or brush strokes, remove objects and run automatic face retouch. Each AI model downloads once, the first time you use the tool.

Deliver it.

JPEG, TIFF, PNG, WebP, JPEG XL and AVIF in sRGB, Display P3, Adobe RGB or ProPhoto RGB, through saved recipes. Output sharpening, watermarks and borders; the queue survives a restart.

New in alpha.2.

Released 15 October 2026, four days after the first alpha. Now with a Linux build.

Linux, at last.

The first Linux build, as an AppImage for x86-64. Develop shows its preview through the app’s web view there, so it is a little less immediate than on a Mac.

Compare, then judge.

Multi view (N) puts up to twelve selected photos side by side with a reference photo, locked zoom and Edit all selected. Before/After (Y) splits the view with a movable divider.

Calmer colour.

Colour noise reduction measured per photo, five grain structures, a three-way grading view, and tone controls that keep tones in order across the whole range.

Relight, experimental.

Add up to four lights to a photo. Rawtonic estimates depth and surfaces on your machine; the models download once, about 1.3 GB. Expect rough edges.

Also: Synchronize Folder finds photos moved, renamed, added or deleted outside Rawtonic; portable libraries with backup and restore; import into folders outside the catalog or add photos where they are; hot folder batches; and many fixes. Full release notes

Install it.

  1. Download the DMG for Apple Silicon, 36 MB. It needs macOS 13 or later; Intel Macs are not supported in this alpha.
  2. Drag Rawtonic to Applications. The alpha is not signed with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS blocks it at first. Clear the quarantine flag once with the command shown here, or right-click the app and choose Open, then Open again.
  3. Keep your backups. It is early software and bugs are expected. Updating from alpha.1? Tone adjustments and luminance noise reduction may look slightly different; your colour noise reduction is kept.
  4. Download the AppImage for x86-64, 131 MB. It runs on Ubuntu 22.04 or later, Debian 12, Fedora and others with glibc 2.35 or later.
  5. Make it executable and start it with the commands shown here. If it does not start, install FUSE 2 (libfuse2t64 on Ubuntu 24.04, libfuse2 on older Ubuntu and Debian) or start it with --appimage-extract-and-run.
  6. Give the first start a minute. Before the first photo appears in Develop, the graphics driver prepares Rawtonic’s shaders. That happens once; later starts are quick.

Questions.

What does it cost? Nothing. Rawtonic is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0-or-later.

Which systems does it run on? macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon, and x86-64 Linux with glibc 2.35 or later. Windows and Intel Macs are not available in this alpha.

Will it change my RAW files? No. Ingest verifies every copy, and edits live in a session and XMP sidecars, never in the RAW file itself.

Why does macOS block the app? This alpha is not signed with an Apple Developer ID. Clear the quarantine flag once, or right-click the app and choose Open, twice.

Do I need an account? No account, no telemetry, no update checks. Rawtonic only goes online when you ask it to. Privacy

Do the AI tools use the cloud? No. They run on your computer. Each model downloads once, the first time you use the tool.

Which cameras open? Most RAW files do. Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don’t open yet; tell us your camera model when a file fails.

Can I bring my Lightroom or Capture One work? Imports from both are possible, but not every adjustment carries over yet.

Can I keep my folders as they are? Yes. Add photos where they are, or import into folders outside the catalog; Synchronize Folder catches changes made elsewhere.

Is it ready for my only copies? It is an early alpha. Rawtonic never modifies your RAW files, but keep backups as you would with any new software.
